1. Two systems are in **thermal contact** if energy can be exchanged between them by heat.
2. Two systems are in **thermal equilibrium** if they are in thermal contact and there is no net exchange of energy.
3. **Heat** is the transfer of energy across the boundary of a system resulting from a temperature difference between the system and its surroundings. The symbol represents the amount of energy transferred. Heat in a system is considered positive _when it is absorbed by the system, increasing its internal energy_. Heat in a system is considered negative _when it is taken out of the system, decreasing its internal energy_.
4. The energy transfer process most closely associated with a temperature difference is called **thermal conduction** or simply **conduction.** The transfer of energy by the movement of a substance is called **convection.** Nor can convection be responsible for any transfer of energy because your hands aren’t above the lamp in the path of convection currents. The warmth felt in your hands must therefore come from the transfer of energy by __radiation__.
5. The __volumetric thermal expansion coefficient__ is approximately three times the __linear thermal expansion coefficient__, and the __area expansion coefficient__ is approximately twice the linear thermal expansion coefficient. $V=3L$, $A=2L$
6. We cannot get more energy out of a system than we put in. $\nabla U=U_{f}-U_{i}=Q+W$ Where _Q_ is the energy transferred, _W_ is the work, and $\nabla U$ is the change in internal energy.
7. _Isobaric_ - Pressure is constant, usually horizontal line. _Adiabatic_ - No heat exchanged with surroundings. _Isovolumetric_ - Volume stays constant, usually vertical line. _Isothermal_ - Temperature stays the same.
8. Constrains the _First Law_, the total entropy of an isolated system always increases over time. A heat engine **cannot convert all the heat it absorbs into useful work**. Some of the heat must always be expelled to a cooler reservoir. The **maximum theoretical efficiency** of a heat engine operating between two thermal reservoirs is given by the **Carnot efficiency** $η=1-\frac{T_{c}}{T_{h}}$
