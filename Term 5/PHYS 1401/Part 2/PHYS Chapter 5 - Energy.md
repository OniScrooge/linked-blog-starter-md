# Formulas
- _Work_ - $F_{x}*\nabla x$ or $W=(F\cos \theta)\nabla x$ depending on direction
- _Kinetic energy_ - $KE=\frac{1}{2}*m*v^2$
- _Work kinetic energy theorem/Net Work_ - $W_{NET}=KE_{f}-KE_{i}=\nabla KE$
	- Accounting for potential energy $W_{nc}=(KE_{f}-KE_{i})+(PE_{f}-PE_{i})$
	- Accounting for friction $W_{nc}=(KE_{f}-KE_{i})+(PE_{f}-PE_{i})+(PE_{sf}-PE_{si})$
- _Calorie_ = $\frac{W_{NET}}{4186}$
- _Hooke's Law_ - $F_{s}=-k*x$
- _Elastic potential energy_ - $PE_{s}=\frac{1}{2}*k*x^2$
	- $k=\frac{N}{m}$
- _Power_ - $P=\frac{W}{t}=F*V$
- _Instantaneous power_ - $P=F*V$
- __Vernier caliper__ - Used to measure linear dimensions like length, depth, and diameter.

# Content
- Forms of energy
	- Mechanical
	- Chemical
	- Electromagnetic
	- Nuclear
	- Contained in mass
- Work
	- _W_ is done by constant force during a linear displacement along the x-axis.
	- Has nothing to do with _time_, _direction_, _velocity, acceleration, etc_
	- Measured in _joules_
	- Work can be added from multiple forces $W=W_{1}+W_{2}+W_{3}+\dots$
- Work applied
	- Not when holding a bucket, no displacement
	- Yes when lowering a box
- Kinetic energy
	- $KE=\frac{1}{2}*m*v^2$
- Work kinetic energy theorem
	- $W_{NET}=KE_{f}-KE_{i}=\nabla KE$
	- Speed will increase if the net work is positive and decrease if the net work is negative
- Forces
	- Conservative - Force can be recovered
		- Work depends only on initial and final positions. IE Gravity, springs, etc.
	- Nonconservative - Force can't be recovered
		- Work moving a force is path-dependent. IE Air drag, etc
- Work Conservative energy theorem - $W_{c}+W_{nc}=\nabla KE$
- Work and Gravitational potential energy - $PE=m*g*y$ & $W_{gravity}=-m*g(y_{f}-y_{i})$
- With this the Work-Energy Theorem can be extended to include potential energy $W_{nc}=(KE_{f}-KE_{i})+(PE_{f}-PE_{i})$
- _Conservation of energy_ - The numerical amount of energy remains constant, though it may change form.
	- $E_{i}=E_{f}$
	- $KE_{i}+PE_{i}=KE_{f}+PE_{f}$
- Suppose three balls are thrown with the same initial speed at a high angle, even angle, and low angle to the ground. Rank the balls in order of speed when they reach the ground.
	- They're the same, no difference. They have the same kinetic energy and undergo the same change.
- _Do not include work done by gravity AND gravitational potential energy_
- Hooke's Law - $F_{s}=-k*x$
	- $F_{s}$ is the restoring force
	- $F_{s}$ is in the opposite direction of x
	- _k_ depends on how the spring was formed, material, wire thickness, etc.
- Elastic potential energy - $PE_{s}=\frac{1}{2}*k*x^2$
	- Required for getting the compressed energy of a spring
- With this the _Work-Energy theorem_ becomes
	- $W_{nc}=(KE_{f}-KE_{i})+(PE_{f}-PE_{i})+(PE_{sf}-PE_{si})$
- Power - $P=\frac{W}{t}=F*V$
	- Often interesting in the rate at which the energy transfer takes place
	- Measured in _Watts_
- Instantaneous power - $P=F*V$
	- Only if force and velocity are parallel
- Power units
	- $1hp (Horse Power) = 550\frac{ft/lb}{s}=746W$
