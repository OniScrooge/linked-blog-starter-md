# Formulas
- _Momentum_: $\vec{p}=m\vec{v}$
- _Alternate KE_: $v_{1f}-v_{2i}=-(v_{1f}-v_{2f})$
- _Impulse_: $\frac{\nabla\vec{p}}{\nabla t}=\frac{m(v_{f}-v_{i})}{\nabla t}=\vec{F}_{NET}$
- _I_: $\vec{I}=\vec{F}\nabla t$
- _Impulse Momentum_: $\vec{I}=\vec{F}\nabla t=\nabla\vec{p}=m_{1}\vec{v_{1f}}-m_{2}\vec{v_{2f}}$
- _Conservation of momentum_: $m_{1}v_{1i}+m_{2}v_{2i}=m_{1}v_{1f}+m_{2}v_{2f}$

# Content
- __Momentum__ is denoted with a $\vec{p}$
	- Has magnitude and direction
	- Momentum components
		- $p_{x}=m*v_{x}$ and $p_{y}=m*v_{y}$
- __Impulse__ is used in order to change a momentum over time.
	- $\frac{\nabla\vec{p}}{\nabla t}=\frac{m(v_{f}-v_{i})}{\nabla t}=\vec{F}_{NET}$
	- Used when a _single, constant force acts on the object_.
		- $\vec{I}=\vec{F}\nabla t$ where __I__ is defined as the _impulse_
- __Impulse-Momentum theorem__ is just both formulae equated
	- $\vec{I}=\vec{F}\nabla t=\nabla\vec{p}=m_{1}\vec{v_{1f}}-m_{2}\vec{v_{2f}}$
	- If two objects have the same and opposite velocity, but different mass, the one with the smaller mass will have a _higher_ final velocity after collision.
- Types of collisions
	- Momentum is conserve in __ANY__ collision
	- Inelastic collisions - Kinetic energy is not conserved
	- Perfectly inelastic collisions - occur when objects stick together
	- Elastic collisions - Momentum and kinetic energy are conserved
	- Actual collisions - Most fall between elastic and perfectly inelastic
- Momentum is a vector
	- Direction is important __MAKE SURE YOU HAVE THE RIGHT SIGNS__
- _Kinetic Energy_ and be simplified in place of the usual KE equation $v_{1f}-v_{2i}=-(v_{1f}-v_{2f})$
